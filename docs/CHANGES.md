# Changes from the submitted version (v1) to the revision (v2)

Each change is linked to the reviewer comment it answers. "v1" = the code behind the manuscript as first submitted.

| # | Area | v1 (submitted) | v2 (revision) | Why | Reviewer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sample split | "Regime-stratified": years with mean range > 80 points (computed on the full sample, test years included) form a high-volatility regime; the first 80% of each regime trains, the last 20% tests. Train = 2009–2017 + 2020–2024, test = 2017–2019 + 2024–2025. | Strictly chronological: train 29 Oct 2009 – 31 Dec 2021 (3,030 days), test 3 Jan 2022 – 25 Nov 2025 (969 days). Every training day precedes every test day. | In v1, 1,175 training days (2020–2024) come after 511 test days (2017–2019), and the regime threshold uses test-period data: look-ahead bias. | #2 |
| 2 | Walk-forward evaluation | 5 expanding-window folds, run for HAR models and 6 selected ML models, as a robustness check. | Same 5 folds, run for all 19 models (HAR-X and the 16 ML models with identical predictors). | Complete, leakage-free comparison across all folds. | #2, #7 |
| 3 | Predictor set | HAR-X: 13 predictors (3 HAR components + session + year + month + weekday + news without `impact_low`). Linear/tree/LSTM: 12 predictors (session + year + month + weekday + all three news dummies + `ny_range_lag1`), no weekly/monthly persistence. | One common set of 12 predictors for HAR-X and all ML models: `har_daily`, `har_weekly`, `har_monthly`, 5 session variables, `month`, `weekday`, `impact_medium`, `impact_high`. HAR and LogHAR keep only the 3 persistence components (benchmarks). | Identical information sets make the comparison valid. | #7, #6 |
| 4 | `year` | Predictor in every ML model and HAR-X; mean importance 42.1% across the seven tree models and 23.0% across the linear models (v1 Tables A2–A3). | Removed from the main specification; tested as an added predictor in `05_robustness.py`. | Under the v1 split, `year` tells the model which regime (and price level) a day belongs to. Under the chronological split its contribution is small: for the linear models and HAR-X it changes test MSE by less than 1% (points specification: DM p ≥ 0.11); for tree models and the LSTM in points it acts as a price-level proxy (e.g. LSTM −9% MSE, Gradient Boosting −5%), and in the scale-free specification, where the level is already removed, it worsens every tree model (see `table9_year_robustness*`). | #6, #9, #2 |
| 5 | News dummies | All three impact dummies plus an intercept in linear models (exact collinearity: the three dummies sum to 1 on every day). | `impact_low` is the omitted reference category in every model. | Removes the dummy-variable trap; coefficients are identified. | — |
| 6 | Hyper-parameter selection | `cv=5` (unshuffled KFold) for Ridge, Lasso, Elastic Net, Adaptive Lasso: some validation folds precede their training data. | `TimeSeriesSplit(5)`. Grids unchanged. | No look-ahead within the training sample. | #2 |
| 7 | Tree models | XGBoost and LightGBM received the test set as `eval_set` (no early stopping, so no effect on forecasts). LightGBM `subsample=0.8` was inactive (requires `subsample_freq>0`). | No test data passed to `fit`. LightGBM `subsample_freq=1`, so the reported `subsample=0.8` is applied. Other hyper-parameters unchanged (Table 4). | Clean separation of test data; code matches the reported configuration. | — |
| 8 | LSTM input window | Window = days t−5 … t−1: the LSTM never saw the same-day session variables that all other models use. Sequences were built on the date-sorted train set and on the date-sorted test set, each of which joins two non-adjacent blocks, so some windows spanned a gap of up to 4.7 years (e.g. 2019-12-31 → 2024-09-17 in the test set). Early-stopping validation = last 20% of training sequences (described as "stratified" in the manuscript). | Window = days t−4 … t (all inputs of day t are known at the open). Sequences built on the full chronological series, then assigned to train/test by target date. Early-stopping validation unchanged (last 20% of training sequences in time order); the manuscript text is corrected. | Same information set as the other models; no windows across gaps. | #7 |
| 9 | LSTM reproducibility | One run; the reported numbers could not be reproduced (three different sets of numbers in the v1 files). | Ensemble of 10 networks with different seeds (5 per walk-forward fold); forecast = ensemble mean; mean ± sd across seeds reported. TensorFlow op determinism enabled. | Reproducible, less dependent on a single random draw. | #4 |
| 10 | Test sample | 800 days for HAR/LSTM, 805 for linear/tree models. | Identical test days for all 19 models. | Like-for-like comparison and DM tests. | #7 |
| 11 | Train vs. test metrics | Test metrics only. | Train R², adjusted R², MAE, RMSE next to test metrics, plus the train–test R² gap. | Overfitting diagnostics. | #4 |
| 12 | Goodness of fit when dropping a predictor | Test R² with and without `year`. | Train adjusted R², test R², change in test MSE and MAE, and DM tests (squared and absolute error) of each model with vs. without `year`, for the 17 models that use the full predictor set. | Out-of-sample R² does not mechanically fall when a predictor is dropped; the DM test gives a formal comparison. | #9 |
| 13 | Feature importance | Different measures per family: |β·σ| for HAR-X, |β| for Elastic Net, gain for CatBoost. | Permutation importance on the test set for all 19 models (one common measure), with group subtotals. Native measures kept as a supplementary table. | Comparable importance across families. | #6 |
| 14 | Diebold–Mariano test | i.i.d. variance of the loss differential, normal p-values, 9 hand-picked pairs, no multiple-testing adjustment. | Newey–West long-run variance (Bartlett, bandwidth ⌊4(T/100)^(2/9)⌋), Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold correction, Student-t p-values; every model vs. HAR, best-of-family pairs and the v1 pairs; Holm-adjusted p-values. | Loss differentials are autocorrelated; many simultaneous tests. | — |
| 15 | Scale-free specification | — | Primary specification of the revision (the points specification becomes a robustness check). Models are estimated on the target and range predictors divided by the trailing 22-day mean range (`har_monthly`); forecasts are rescaled to index points and all metrics are computed in points, so the forecast target is unchanged. 11 common predictors (`har_monthly` becomes the normaliser); the HAR benchmark regresses the ratio target on `har_daily/har_monthly` and `har_weekly/har_monthly` with an intercept, which in points is a HAR without constant. | The mean NY range rose from 17 points (2009) to 213 points (2025) together with the index level; tree models and the LSTM cannot extrapolate beyond the training range. | #6, #7 |
| 16 | Code and data availability | Notebooks only, no repository. | This repository: one shared module (`src/nqvol`) used by every script, pinned library versions, data dictionary and the list of excluded days. | Replicability. | #1 |
| 17 | Session summaries vs individual bars | Session variables used as summaries (`asia_range`, `london_range`) without justification. | `scripts/07_raw_bars.py` replaces (B) or augments (S+B) the two session ranges with the ranges of the 15 underlying 30-minute bars (9 Asia, 6 London) for HAR-X, Elastic Net, Random Forest, CatBoost and LightGBM, on the main split and the 5 walk-forward folds, with DM tests against the summary set. The bars reproduce all four session variables of `ml_dataset1.csv` on 4,021/4,021 days. | Tests empirically whether aggregation loses forecasting information; in the primary scale-free specification no model improves significantly on the summaries (DM p ≥ 0.08), at 13–15 extra predictors; in the points specification only the boosted trees gain (LightGBM, DM p < 0.01), the linear models lose out of sample. | #3 |
| 18 | Sample description | Sample rule not stated. | Inclusion rule and the 146 excluded weekdays (134 of them exchange holidays and shortened sessions) documented in `data/README.md` and listed with reasons in `data/excluded_days.csv` (`scripts/00_excluded_days.py`). | Transparency of the sample. | #1 |
